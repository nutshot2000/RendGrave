Pixel Physics Lab

A falling-sand cellular automaton with chunks, dirty rectangles, reactions, free particles and pixel rigid bodies. Burn, dissolve or blow a hole through a rigid body and it splits into separate pieces that fall on their own.

Hover the level to inspect a cell.
Materials
Rigid bodies

Rigid bodies: drag to draw a beam, click for a round chunk. Brush size sets the thickness. Fixed wood above is anchored scenery and never falls; use Wood body for anything that should break off and drop.

Wand

Press where the wand should be, drag to aim, and hold to keep casting. Cards fire left to right. Modifiers (blue) apply to every projectile in the same cast; Double and Triple cast (purple) pull the next 2 or 3 projectiles into one volley. After the last card the wand recharges. Click a wand card to remove it, a library card to add it.

Reading the overlay
  • Faint grid: 32×32-cell chunks. The world is 320×180 cells, 60 chunks.
  • Outlined chunk: awake this frame. Sleeping chunks cost nothing.
  • Blue box: the dirty rectangle. Only these cells get scanned.
  • Green outline: a rigid body's shape. The faint line is the full marching-squares trace; the bright line keeps only the points Douglas–Peucker needs (the squares). Dashed means asleep. The dot is the centre of mass, the line its velocity.
  • Red dot and line: the contact point and surface normal used to resolve a collision.
  • Amber ring: a free particle, flying outside the grid until it lands.
  • Dashed amber line: the wand's aim while you hold to cast.

Each frame every awake body is lifted out of the grid, moved by its physics, then stamped back in at its new position and angle. Between those steps the body's pixels are ordinary cells, so fire, acid and explosions act on them. A body that loses pixels is split into its connected pieces.
